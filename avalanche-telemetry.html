Why Beacons Aren't Enough

A beacon finds a location.
It doesn't find who's critical.

Standard 457 kHz transceivers do one job well: they get a rescuer standing on top of a buried victim. They say nothing about condition.

Most burials involve more than one victim, found in sequence, not at once. Right now, triage order is set by whoever gets dug out first — not by who's actually running out of time.

This system doesn't replace the beacon. It rides alongside it, closing that gap with live physiological signal.

Illustrative — outcome uncertainty rises the longer triage runs blind
System Architecture

Two channels.
One wearable.

The 457 kHz beacon stays exactly as it is — untouched, still the primary locator. A secondary LoRa channel carries live vitals from the wearable straight to the rescuer's receiver.

Power Architecture

Dual-wake keeps
the battery alive.

The wearable only spends power when it needs to — waking instantly on a fall, or checking in on a fixed schedule otherwise.

IMU Fall-Event Trigger

An abrupt orientation or acceleration change wakes the unit immediately and starts continuous transmission.

5-Minute Periodic Check

Absent a fall event, the unit wakes on a timer, samples vitals, transmits, and sleeps again to conserve power.
